Website quotes vary by an order of magnitude for what sounds like the same brief, and that is genuinely confusing when you are the one buying. Here is what actually sits behind the numbers.

## The ranges

| What you are buying | Typical range |
| --- | --- |
| Template-based marketing site, light customisation | $1,000 – $4,000 |
| Custom-designed marketing site, CMS, 8–15 pages | $5,000 – $15,000 |
| Web platform: auth, dashboards, integrations | $20,000 – $50,000 |
| Multi-role product with custom backend | $50,000+ |

Those are build costs. Budget separately for hosting, domains, and a maintenance retainer.

## What actually moves the number

**1. Design originality.** Adapting a proven layout is fast. A distinctive, animated, art-directed experience is a separate discipline with its own timeline. This is usually the biggest single swing.

**2. Content readiness.** Sites stall on copy far more often than on code. If we are writing and sourcing content, that is scope. If it is ready on day one, the project moves.

**3. Integrations.** CRM, payment provider, booking system, ERP, legacy database — each one is discovery, build, error handling, and testing. Three integrations is not three times one integration; it is more.

**4. Custom logic.** A pricing calculator, a quoting tool, a customer portal. Anything with rules is software, not pages, and it gets estimated as software.

**5. Performance and accessibility targets.** "Looks fine" is cheap. Passing Core Web Vitals and WCAG AA on every template is deliberate work.

Notice what is not on that list: page count. Ten pages sharing one design system cost barely more than six.

## Where money gets wasted

- **Designing before deciding.** Signing off visuals before the content strategy exists guarantees a redesign mid-build.
- **Rebuilding what you could buy.** Custom search, custom auth, custom analytics — occasionally justified, usually not.
- **No design system.** Without shared components, page eleven costs as much as page one.
- **Scope by accumulation.** "While we're in there" is how a $9,000 project becomes a $19,000 one.

## How to get a quote you can compare

Send three things to every agency you approach:

1. Your goal, in a sentence — leads, sales, credibility, support deflection.
2. A list of page types, not pages ("service page", "case study", "pricing").
3. Every system it has to talk to.

With that, quotes become comparable. Without it, you are comparing guesses.
